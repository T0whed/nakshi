How the engine works

  
The insight is that nobody needs to be persuaded to collect this data. Factories already generate all of it — as scans, photocopies, WhatsApp images and Excel files scattered across a compliance officer's desk. Nakshi does not ask for a new data discipline. It reads what exists.

  

    
STAGE 01
Ingest
Whatever the factory already has: scanned certificates, phone photos of paper bills, buyer PDFs, mill Excel files.
layout‑aware OCR
Bangla + English + Bangla numerals

    
STAGE 02
Extract
A vision‑language model pulls typed fields with per‑field confidence, so a human only reviews what the model is unsure about.
fine‑tuned VLM
human‑in‑the‑loop below 0.85

    
STAGE 03
Reconcile
The defensible core. Build the bill‑of‑materials graph, then test every claim against the certificates that are supposed to back it.
mass balance · validity windows
consumption · tier depth

A passport record and a GS1 Digital Link QR — plus auto-filled buyer questionnaires from the same single source of truth.

Why this, why here, why now

  

    

      
The squeeze

      
    
        
  • RMG is 80.6% of Bangladesh's exports — Tk 4.76 lakh crore in FY2025‑26 — and the EU takes roughly half of it.
    • 
        
  • LDC graduation is set for 24 Nov 2026; a three‑year deferral to 2029 was backed by ECOSOC in July 2026 and still awaits a UNGA vote.
    • 
        
  • Either way, duty‑free EBA access ends and apparel faces 9.6% standard‑GSP or full MFN. Cheapest by default stops working.
    • 
      

    

    

      
The unavoidable deadline

      
    
        
  • The EU's ESPR textile delegated act is expected late 2027, with passport obligations roughly 18 months later.
    • 
        
  • The data a passport needs sits with the factory, not the brand — which is exactly why brands will push the cost down the chain.
    • 
        
  • The unsold‑stock destruction ban already bit large EU firms in July 2026. This is not a hypothetical calendar.
    • 
      

    

    

      
The pain that pays today

      
    
        
  • A factory serving ten buyers maintains amfori BSCI, Sedex SMETA, Higg FEM, SLCP, WRAP and bespoke buyer questionnaires — the same facts, re‑entered by hand, 15+ times a year.
    • 
        
  • Nakshi sells audit‑preparation hours saved now, and gets paid for passports in 2029. The wedge funds the moat.
    •

Beachhead: the ~1,200 EU‑facing export factories. At Tk 60,000/mo average that is Tk 86 crore ARR before leaving Bangladesh — then Vietnam, India, Türkiye and Cambodia, where the regulation is identical and the pain is the same. Converted at Tk 122.9 to the US dollar (3 Sept 2026); export earnings are settled in dollars, so the taka figures move with the rate.

    

    

      
Why it compounds

      
    
        
  • Factories share suppliers. The same few hundred dyeing and finishing mills serve everyone, so each factory onboarded enriches the graph for the next — a certificate verified once is verified for all.
    • 
        
  • A Bangla‑and‑English document corpus of this kind exists nowhere else, and cannot be bought.
    • 
        
  • European compliance vendors have the data model but no ground truth here. Local software has the relationships but no model. Nakshi needs both.
    • 
      

    

    

      
The national case

      
    
        
  • If Bangladesh becomes the first exporting nation with a working garment traceability layer, graduation stops being purely a loss.
    • 
        
  • Compliance turns from a cost the country absorbs into the reason a buyer chooses Dhaka over Hanoi.
    • 
        
  • Built as a company, usable as public infrastructure — the BGMEA/BKMEA membership rolls are the distribution channel.
    •
